
1. Space-Time Tile Reservations (MAPF)

• The Bug/Bottleneck: Your choose_pearl matching system ensures dragons don't pick the same goal, but plan_bfs runs independently. When multiple feeders rush to the Queen during the HARVEST phase, or when collectors cross narrow paths, they step on each other, causing stalls or blocking paths.
• The Fix: Create a shared global space-time reservation table std::vector<std::vector<int>> reservationMap(N, std::vector<int>(MAX_SPRINT, -1)) mapped to future rounds. When high-priority units (Queen, Giants, or high-tier Leaders) plan a path, they reserve (cell, round). Lower-priority units must treat those space-time coordinates as impassable walls in their BFS loop, ensuring clear pathways for critical bots.

2. Tail-First Approach for the Endgame "Feeder Funnel"

• The Bug/Bottleneck: In decide(), your HARVEST logic allows nearby dragons to die within a Chebyshev distance of 3 (HARVEST_DIE_DIST) of the Queen's head. Rushing her head often fills up the open tiles she needs to escape enemy snipers, trapping her.
• The Fix: Force feeding units to path toward the Queen's tail instead of her head. Because you keep a precise time-aware model of your own segments (ownRel), you know exactly when her tail moves. Feeders should queue up behind her path of movement, dying exactly where her tail is about to leave a tile, allowing her to vacuum up the drops safely from behind while keeping her front vectors wide open.

3. Asymmetry Trapping & Anomaly Validation

• The Bug/Bottleneck: You mirror map facts instantly under set_symmetry. High-level opponents will intentionally alter map layout metadata (like creating asymmetrical kelp blockades or breaking symmetry patterns in valid ways if the map rules allow subtle variances) to feed false metrics into your world model.
• The Fix: Implement an Anomaly Detection Layer. If symKnown is set, but a high-priority scout enters a mirrored cell and finds a mismatch (e.g., expected open water but hits a wall/kelp), immediately trigger a symFailed = true override. Wipe the mirrored assumptions from your world grid and switch to an aggressive, purely empirical exploration mode.

4. Dynamic Threat-Responsive Role Weights

• The Bug/Bottleneck: Your roles (isHunter, isGuard, isScreen) are rigidly distributed using a static ID hash (hunter_slot). If you outnumber the enemy 10-to-2, you waste units idling on guard duty. If you are outnumbered, your fixed hunters execute suicidal tracking runs.
• The Fix: Replace hardcoded HUNTER_SHARE and GUARD_SHARE constants with an environment-driven variable calculated inside compute_fleet_state().
	• Calculate a Global Pressure Ratio: \(\text{Ratio} = \frac{\text{Enemy Est. Strength}}{\text{Our Living Units}}\).
	• If Ratio < 0.5 (Domination), drop guards entirely and shift 90% of the fleet to aggressive hunting.
	• If Ratio > 2.0 (Surrounded), retract all hunters into a spherical screen configuration to act as physical blast shields for the Queen.

5. Multi-Turn Adversarial Horizon Lookahead (MCTS)

• The Bug/Bottleneck: Your QUEEN_LOOK2 logic looks ahead by 2 moves to keep the Queen safe, but frame-by-frame pathing calculations can be baited. Smart bots will position a long unit to simulate a trap, forcing your Queen to switch directions repeatedly and pin herself.
• The Fix: Implement a micro-sized Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) or a 5-to-6 turn Alpha-Beta Minimax tree exclusively for the Queen when an enemy head is within 6 tiles. Limit the tree depth to her immediate 7x7 local grid to save processing budget. Simulate the worst-case movement options of the closest enemy head to guarantee the Queen paths toward long-term open loops.

6. Value-Based "Paid-Step Arbitrage"

• The Bug/Bottleneck: Your PAID_PEARL_RACE code allows a non-queen bot to pay precisely one body segment if it helps them win a pearl race. This flat calculation values all pearls and all opponents equally.
• The Fix: Switch to an Arbitrage Utility Score. Allow your combat units to pay 2 or even 3 segments if the calculation proves that doing so will starve an enemy leader or force a head-on collision that kills a massive enemy unit. The investment cost of losing segments is highly profitable if it successfully eliminates a high-value target or secures a critical spawn area.

7. Strategic Chokepoint Grid Masking

• The Bug/Bottleneck: Your choke_post_search dynamically recalculates bridges to position guards. However, it treats all pathways into your side of the map as uniform paths.
• The Fix: Build a permanent static topology layout map on Round 0. Identify absolute chokepoints—narrow pathways that completely bisect the map. Instead of your guards tracking the Queen's position flexibly (GUARD_RING), anchor them to these structural bottlenecks. Blocking these strategic points early acts as an iron curtain, containing the enemy on their half of the map while your team cleans up the rest of the grid.

8. Deep Unexplored Portal Probing (Scout Chains)

• The Bug/Bottleneck: Your PORTAL_SCOUTS algorithm lets short, single units blindly cross portals to discover map links. If the far end opens into a highly congested or hostile zone, the unit dies instantly, providing minimal value.
• The Fix: Implement Scout Chains. When a portal role bot identifies an unlocated far side, assign a trailing buddy bot to follow exactly 2 rounds behind it. If the lead scout is instantly destroyed, the trailing bot registers the drop coordinates via its sonar map update, logging the threat profile of the far side without jumping into the trap itself.
