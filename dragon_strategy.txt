BATTLECODE DRAGON STRATEGY

1. CORE OBJECTIVE

The overall objective is to maximize the number of dragons early, aggressively collect pearls, explore the map as quickly as possible, gather and distribute information through sonar, and eventually concentrate resources into a few very large leader dragons.

The strategy should remain aggressive rather than passive:
- Collect pearls whenever they are reachable.
- Explore portals, kelps, boundaries, and the overall map early.
- Continuously distribute useful information between our dragons.
- Avoid sending all dragons toward the same pearl.
- Use pathfinding to choose efficient routes.
- Use symmetry to predict pearl, portal, and kelp locations.
- Adapt the strategy based on the number and behavior of opponent dragons.

2. EARLY-GAME DRAGON SPLITTING

At the beginning of the game, if a dragon has size >= 4 and splitting is possible, split immediately.

The main purpose is to maximize the total number of dragons as quickly as possible.

Whenever possible:
- Size >= 4 -> split into two dragons.
- Continue splitting aggressively during the initial phase.
- The goal is to gain a numerical majority quickly.

This early splitting should continue for a predefined number X of rounds.

3. UNAVOIDABLE-DEATH SPLIT

If a dragon is going to die and death is certain, use the special split strategy:

- Split into a size-2 dragon and a size-(X-2) dragon.
- The size-2 dragon is expected to die.
- The size-(X-2) dragon survives.
- Transfer/make the head of the size-(X-2) dragon at the tail of the original dragon as required by the game mechanics.

The purpose is to sacrifice the minimum amount of length necessary while preserving the maximum possible length.

This strategy should be used whenever death is unavoidable, including situations involving dangerous collisions or unavoidable hazards.

4. SONAR AND INFORMATION SHARING

Sonar is one of the most important components of the strategy.

Use sonar every round for all four directions:
- North
- South
- East
- West

The objective is to continuously decode the surrounding map and build a shared understanding of the environment.

Each dragon should use sonar to identify:
- Pearls
- Opponent dragons
- Kelps
- Portals
- Portal structures
- Important map boundaries/areas
- Potential future pearl locations
- Useful information discovered by other dragons

When one dragon discovers important information, share it through sonar with the other dragons whenever possible.

Information about dangerous encounters should also be shared:
- If a dragon encounters an opponent and gets killed, communicate that information.
- If a dragon discovers that a particular area is dangerous, communicate it.
- If a dragon discovers a pearl or portal, communicate its location and relevant timing information.

The goal is to develop a global overview of the map as early as possible.

5. PORTAL EXPLORATION

Portals should be explored aggressively.

Whenever a dragon encounters a portal:
- Enter/explore the portal when appropriate.
- Decode the structure of the portal.
- Determine where the portal leads.
- Determine what the environment inside looks like.
- Identify pearls inside or around the portal.
- Determine when pearls will appear.
- Store information about the portal for future use.

The dragon exploring the portal should communicate the discovered information to the rest of the dragon fleet through sonar.

Portal information should be stored so that other dragons can use it later without needing to rediscover the portal.

If a portal contains many pearls or significant opportunities for gaining size, send additional dragons there based on the available information.

However, do not automatically send every dragon to the same portal. Distribution should depend on:
- Number of pearls
- Pearl timing
- Current dragon sizes
- Opponent presence
- Risk
- Distance
- Available routes

6. AGGRESSIVE PEARL HUNTING

Pearls are a major priority.

The strategy should aggressively search for and collect pearls throughout the entire game.

Whenever a pearl is detected:
- Determine whether one of our dragons is already assigned to it.
- If not, assign an appropriate dragon.
- Use pathfinding to determine an efficient route.
- Send only a limited number of dragons toward that pearl.
- Never send the entire fleet to the same pearl.

The objective is to maximize total pearl collection while maintaining broad map coverage.

If multiple pearls exist in different directions, distribute dragons among them rather than concentrating everyone on one location.

7. PATHFINDING FOR PEARLS

Use pathfinding algorithms to determine:
- Which dragon should collect a pearl.
- Which route that dragon should take.
- Whether another dragon can reach the pearl faster.
- Whether the route is safe.
- Whether splitting can make reaching the pearl more efficient.

Pearl assignment should consider:
- Distance
- Direction the dragon is currently facing/moving
- Dragon size
- Nearby opponents
- Other available pearls
- Expected future pearl positions
- Portal locations
- Risk of death

Avoid duplicate assignments.

If a pearl already has an assigned dragon and another dragon can collect a different pearl, send the second dragon elsewhere.

8. PEARL COLLECTION USING SPLITTING

Whenever a pearl is detected, make sure a dragon is sent to collect it.

If the dragon is already facing toward the pearl, it can travel directly.

If the dragon is facing away from the pearl and reaching the pearl through normal movement would be inefficient or impossible, use splitting when appropriate.

For example:
- Split a size-X dragon into size-2 and size-(X-2).
- Send the size-2 dragon toward the pearl.
- Send the size-(X-2) dragon away to preserve the majority of the length.

The small dragon can be sacrificed if necessary to obtain the pearl.

After collecting the pearl, if that dragon is going to die, again use the size-2 + size-(X-2) split to preserve as much length as possible.

The key rule is:

Whenever a reachable pearl is identified, attempt to collect it, either directly or through a split-based strategy.

9. MAP COVERAGE AND DRAGON DISTRIBUTION

Do not concentrate all dragons in one location.

The fleet should cover:
- Different directions
- Different regions
- Different pearl locations
- Different portal locations
- Different portions of the map

This increases:
- Information gathering
- Pearl collection
- Opponent detection
- Map exploration
- Control of the board

When a pearl is seen in one area, send an appropriate number of dragons there, but retain enough dragons elsewhere to continue exploring.

10. SYMMETRY

Map symmetry is extremely important.

Use observed map symmetry to predict:
- Where additional pearls may appear.
- Where portals may be located.
- Where kelps may be located.
- Where useful resources may appear.
- Where unexplored opportunities may exist.

If a pearl is found at one position and the map is symmetric, investigate/send a dragon toward the corresponding symmetric position because a matching pearl may appear there.

Do not rely only on direct observation. Use known symmetry to predict future opportunities.

This can reduce exploration time and allow dragons to reach resources before they are directly observed.

11. LEADER DRAGON PHASE

After the initial splitting phase, select approximately 2-3 dragons to become leader dragons.

Leader dragons should stop splitting aggressively.

Their primary purpose becomes increasing their size and eventually producing very large dragons.

The exact round at which this transition happens should depend on the number and behavior of opponent dragons.

The leaders should:
- Collect pearls aggressively.
- Grow as large as possible.
- Avoid unnecessary splitting.
- Receive information from other dragons.
- Use the information collected by the fleet.
- Become the primary large dragons for the late-game strategy.

12. ADAPTIVE LEADER TIMING

The number of opponents and how aggressively they approach us determines when to transition from maximizing dragon count to maximizing dragon size.

Suggested approximate transition points:

- Very many opponent dragons / high danger:
  Start leader-growth phase around round 400.

- Moderately high opponent presence:
  Start around round 350-370.

- Lower opponent presence:
  Start around round 300-320.

- Very few opponent dragons:
  Start around round 250.

- Almost no opponent dragons:
  Start around round 200.

These are initial heuristic values and should eventually be adjusted based on actual map behavior and performance.

The important principle is:

More dangerous opponents -> maintain a larger dragon fleet for longer.

Fewer dangerous opponents -> begin growing the maximum-size dragons earlier.

13. LOW-OPPONENT-AREA STRATEGY

If a particular area of the map contains very few opponent dragons, use a special farming strategy.

This strategy should only be activated when:
- We see very few opponent dragons in the area.
- Opponents are not realistically able to reach the area.
- Opponents are separated from us by kelps/boundaries or other obstacles.
- The area can be considered relatively safe.

In this situation:
- Select one leader dragon.
- Use the other dragons in that area to collect pearls.
- If appropriate, sacrifice/collide those smaller dragons so that their pearls are released.
- Send the pearl information to the leader through sonar.
- Let the leader collect the released pearls.
- Continue feeding the leader with pearls.
- Maximize the leader's size.

The goal is to convert many smaller dragons into resources for one extremely large dragon.

14. WHEN NOT TO USE THE LOW-OPPONENT FARMING STRATEGY

Do NOT use the farming strategy if many opponent dragons are approaching and can realistically reach our area.

If opponents are actively coming toward us:
- Maintain a larger number of dragons.
- Avoid sacrificing too many dragons.
- Preserve numerical strength.
- Keep enough dragons available for defensive and offensive collisions.
- Do not allow the maximum-size dragon to become an easy target.

The strategy should switch dynamically between:
A. Farming/growth mode
B. Fleet-preservation mode
C. Aggressive combat mode

15. OPPONENT DENSITY AND AGGRESSION

Opponent visibility should be continuously monitored.

If we see many opponent dragons in an area and they can reach us, assume that collisions are likely.

In high-opponent areas:
- Become aggressive.
- Send some dragons toward opponents.
- Attempt head-on collisions when advantageous.
- Try to eliminate opponent dragons.
- Simultaneously continue collecting pearls.
- Do not send the entire fleet into combat.
- Keep enough dragons alive to maintain numerical advantage and resource collection.

The objective is to reduce opponent dragon count while increasing our own dragon count through pearl collection and splitting.

16. COLLISION STRATEGY

When opponents are approaching and collision opportunities are favorable:
- Use some dragons for offensive collisions.
- Keep other dragons collecting pearls.
- Maintain broad map coverage.
- Avoid committing every dragon to the same fight.

The fleet should be divided into roles:
- Combat dragons
- Pearl collectors
- Explorers
- Portal explorers
- Leader dragons

These roles can change dynamically depending on the situation.

17. INFORMATION AFTER DEATH

If one of our dragons dies:
- Record the location.
- Record what caused the death.
- Determine whether an opponent was involved.
- Determine whether a hazard/portal/kelp/boundary was involved.
- Communicate the information through sonar.

Other dragons should use this information to avoid repeating the same mistake.

Death locations can also provide information about opponent movement and dangerous map regions.

18. FUTURE PEARL TIMING

The strategy should store information about when pearls appear.

For example, if a particular area follows a predictable pearl cycle:
- Record the appearance timing.
- Predict the next appearance.
- Position a suitable dragon nearby.
- Send it toward the area at the correct time.
- Avoid unnecessarily sending the whole fleet.

This is especially important for portal regions and recurring pearl locations.

19. EXAMPLE: SEVEN + SEVEN / KNOWN PEARL TIMING

For areas where the timing and position of the next pearl are known:

- Determine when the next pearl will appear.
- Determine whether one of our dragons is already positioned to collect it.
- If a suitable dragon is facing the correct direction, send it directly.
- If the dragon is facing the opposite direction and cannot efficiently reach the pearl, use a split.
- Send the size-2 portion toward the pearl.
- Send the size-(X-2) portion away to preserve the majority of the length.
- Collect the pearl.
- If the collecting dragon is subsequently going to die, use another size-2 + size-(X-2) split to preserve the larger portion.

The principle remains: do not leave an available pearl uncollected when a viable collection strategy exists.

20. GLOBAL STRATEGY LOOP

Every round, the strategy should broadly perform the following:

1. Use sonar in all four directions.
2. Update the global map model.
3. Detect pearls.
4. Detect opponents.
5. Detect kelps.
6. Detect portals.
7. Update portal information.
8. Update known pearl timings.
9. Predict symmetric resource locations.
10. Assign dragons to pearls.
11. Use pathfinding to determine routes.
12. Avoid duplicate pearl assignments.
13. Check whether any dragon is going to die.
14. If death is unavoidable, use the size-2 + size-(X-2) survival split.
15. Check whether the early-game splitting phase is still active.
16. If size >= 4 and early splitting is active, split whenever possible.
17. Determine opponent density and danger.
18. Determine whether to use farming, combat, or fleet-preservation mode.
19. Protect leader dragons when opponents are nearby.
20. Continue collecting pearls aggressively.

21. HIGH-LEVEL PHASES

PHASE 1 — EARLY EXPANSION
- Split aggressively.
- Maximize number of dragons.
- Explore the map.
- Use sonar continuously.
- Find portals.
- Find pearls.
- Discover symmetry.
- Build the initial map model.

PHASE 2 — RESOURCE DOMINANCE
- Continue aggressive pearl collection.
- Assign dragons efficiently.
- Avoid duplicate pearl targeting.
- Use pathfinding.
- Predict future pearls.
- Use portal information.
- Maintain broad map coverage.

PHASE 3 — ADAPTIVE TRANSITION
- Evaluate opponent density.
- Evaluate how many opponents can actually reach us.
- Decide when to stop aggressive splitting.
- Select 2-3 leader dragons.
- Begin concentrating resources into leaders.

PHASE 4 — LEADER GROWTH
- Leaders stop unnecessary splitting.
- Smaller dragons continue exploration/resource collection.
- Feed information and pearls toward leaders.
- Use safe regions for farming.
- Avoid exposing leaders to unnecessary collisions.

PHASE 5 — COMBAT / ENDGAME
- If opponents are numerous and approaching, use aggressive collision tactics.
- Eliminate opponents where advantageous.
- Preserve enough dragons for numerical strength.
- Continue collecting pearls.
- Grow leader dragons as large as possible.
- Use unavoidable-death splitting to preserve maximum length.

22. CORE PRINCIPLES

The strategy can be summarized by these rules:

1. Split early and aggressively when size >= 4.
2. Maximize the number of dragons in the early game.
3. Use sonar every round in all four directions.
4. Build a shared global map.
5. Explore every useful portal and remember its structure.
6. Hunt pearls aggressively.
7. Never send all dragons toward one pearl.
8. Avoid duplicate pearl assignments.
9. Use pathfinding for efficient pearl collection.
10. Exploit map symmetry.
11. Predict future pearl locations and timings.
12. If death is unavoidable, sacrifice size 2 to preserve size X-2.
13. Adapt the transition to leader growth based on opponent density.
14. Few reachable opponents -> grow leaders earlier.
15. Many reachable opponents -> maintain more dragons for longer.
16. In safe areas, use smaller dragons to feed pearls to a leader.
17. Do not use the farming strategy when opponents can reach the area.
18. When many opponents approach, become aggressive and seek head-on collisions.
19. Share death and collision information through sonar.
20. Always remain active: explore, collect pearls, gather information, and adapt.

23. FINAL STRATEGIC IDEA

The central idea is to first win through NUMBERS and INFORMATION, then transition into SIZE and CONTROL.

Early game:
NUMBER OF DRAGONS + MAP INFORMATION + PEARLS

Mid game:
PEARL CONTROL + MAP CONTROL + ADAPTIVE COMBAT

Late game:
LARGE LEADER DRAGONS + RESOURCE CONCENTRATION + OPPONENT ELIMINATION

The strategy should never be completely fixed. The number of opponents, their movement, accessibility of regions, pearl locations, portal structure, map symmetry, and danger level should continuously determine whether we prioritize:

- More dragons
- Larger dragons
- More pearls
- More exploration
- More combat
- More information
- Leader growth

The key objective throughout the match is to maximize our total advantage while minimizing unnecessary deaths and inefficient dragon movement.
