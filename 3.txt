DEATH

A dragon dies when its head collides with something, or when its program
fails to produce a valid action on its turn.

Causes of Death

Reason | Cause Hit Wall | Moved into kelp. Hit Self | Moved into its own
body, including its tail. Hit Other Body | Moved into a segment of
another dragon from any team. Head to Head | Moved into another dragon’s
head, causing both dragons to die. No Valid Action | Requested a sprint
it could not pay for, requested an illegal split, produced a malformed
command, or output no action.

Mandatory Action

The engine treats a turn with no valid action as suicide. Any dragon
whose program crashes or outputs nothing (or garbage) will be killed.
Dragons which fail to end their turn before the time limit but have
output at least one valid action will experience a different fate (see
Timeouts).

PEARL DROPPING

Upon death, dragons exhibit a startling phenomenon: starting from their
head, every second body segment crystallises into pearls. A dragon of
length L therefore drops ⌊L/2⌋ pearls.

GAME FORMAT

By facing off with competitor teams through ranked and unranked games,
you will get an ELO score that determines your spot on the leaderboard.
Your ELO score increases when you win games, and decreases when you lose
games. ELO System sets out the arithmetic and the tiers.

Ranked and unranked

A ranked battle consists of five games and will cause team ELOs to
update. Maps are chosen at random by the competition server. Ranked
battles come from two places: the ones you request, and the autoscrim
draws below, which are ranked as well. In an unranked battle, teams may
choose to verse another team on particular maps; one game is played for
each map. Unranked battles do not update ELOs nor ranks. Your team keeps
the same colour across every game of a battle, and after each battle you
will be able to see the replays.

Requesting a battle (‘scrim’)

You can request a battle against a specific team on the leaderboard or
from a team’s page in find a team. The battle outcome appears in All
battles and in Your battles. Note that all battles are publicly
viewable, so be wise about concealing your strategy!

Both teams need an active submission. Unranked battles are then open to
every team; ranked ones need the switch under Settings on both Team
pages, and must target a team rated no more than 50 below you, although
anyone above you is fair game. That switch waits eight hours between
flips, so nobody can duck a rating by turning it off between requests.
You cannot start a second battle against a team while one is still
pending or running. Your team may start 60 games an hour by hand, which
is 12 ranked scrims.

Battles you are drawn into (‘autoscrim’)

Autoscrim battles are ranked. They move your rating exactly as a ranked
battle you asked for does, and most of the games your team plays will be
these, so your place on the ladder is mostly decided by them.

You do not have to ask for every battle. Every two hours, on the UTC
boundary, the ladder is sorted and each team is drawn an opponent from
the eight places above and the eight below, so you meet teams near your
own rating. The draw favours opponents you have not played for a while,
and over a day you see most of the ladder around you.

You are in the draw whenever you have an active submission; the ranked
switch in your settings governs only the battles teams request. Each
draw is a ranked five-game series on five maps chosen at random, at most
one per team per round, and it does not spend your hourly allowance.

Submitting a bot

Using either the CLI or the website, you may upload and submit code.
Once submissions are made, they cannot be deleted. Only one submission
can be ‘active’ at a time. Your ‘active’ submission is the one that the
game server will use for any incoming / outgoing battles.

ELO SYSTEM

There is one ladder and one rating per team, starting at 1500. Every
ranked battle moves both teams along it; unranked battles leave it
alone.

What a rating means

A rating is a prediction. Put two of them side by side and Elo turns the
gap into an expected share of the battle’s five games. Two equal teams
are predicted to split them 2.5 each. A team 400 points ahead is
predicted to take about 4.5 of the 5.

When the battle finishes, what actually happened is compared with that
prediction. If you outperform relative to your Elo, you take rating off
the other team; if you fall short, you lose Elo. The two changes are
equal and opposite, so the total on the ladder never moves.

This is why a rating eventually settles somewhere. If you sit above the
level you actually play at, you will keep losing ELO until your results
support where you are.

Why a win can cost you rating

Your score in a battle is your proportion of games won, not whether you
won overall. A ranked battle is five games, so a 3–2 win counts as 0.6,
not 1.

Against a team 400 points below you, the prediction is about 4.5 games
of the 5. Win 3–2 and you took 3, well short of what your rating claimed
you would do, so rating moves to them even though you won the battle. At
that gap even 4–1 costs you, and only a 5–0 sweep gains anything.

The same rule is what makes playing upwards worth it. An underdog
predicted to take half a game is already ahead of the prediction with
one win, so a 1–4 loss gains rating.

Examples

Your rating | Opponent | Predicted | Result | Change 1500 | 1500 | 2.5

5 | 3–2 | −30 1500 | 1900 | 0.5 of 5 | 2–3 | +30

Equal teams, won 3–2. The prediction is an even 2.5 games each, and you
took half a game more than that, so +10 changes hands. A close battle
between equals barely moves either of you.

Equal teams, won 5–0. The same prediction, but there was nothing left to
take. That is as far past an even prediction as a result can get, so you
collect +48, the biggest swing two equal teams can produce.

400 points ahead, won 3–2. Your rating predicted 4.5 games of the 5, and
3 is well under it. You won the battle and still paid 30 rating for it.
The favourite is not being punished for winning; it is being charged for
winning by less than it claimed it would.

400 points behind, lost 2–3. The inverse of the row above. You were
predicted to take 0.5 games, took 2, and +30 comes to you despite losing
the battle.

The formula

Ratings are updated by Elo with K = 96, applied once per battle rather
than per game. Against a team rated R_B, a team rated R_A has expected
share 1 / (1 + 10^((R_B − R_A) / 400)). Its actual share is the fraction
of the battle’s games it won, with a draw counting as half. The rating
changes by K times the difference, rounded, and the two teams’ changes
sum to zero.

A battle is settled only when all of its games have finished, so a
five-game series changes the rating once. Unranked battles do not affect
ratings. The autoscrim draw is ranked, so those battles move your rating
too, and they are most of what you play.

THE CLI

unswbc creates a bot project, builds it, plays matches and uploads the
result. The commands below are in the order you will need them.
unswbc help <command> lists the options for any one of them.

Recommended

unswbc is a Python package. Install uv if you do not have it, open a new
shell so it is on your PATH, then install the toolkit. Later,
uv tool upgrade unswbc takes the newest version and
uv tool install unswbc==0.3.0 pins one.

uv tool install unswbc

If you don’t have uv

pip installs it just as well, given Python 3.11 or newer, and puts
unswbc on your PATH. Debian, Ubuntu and Homebrew refuse a plain pip
install and say externally-managed-environment; on those, uv is the way.

pip install unswbc

unswbc

On its own it lists the commands and checks this machine: the Python
interpreter, the C and C++ compilers, VS Code and the replay viewer.
Anything missing is named with the step that installs it.

unswbc init

Creates a project in c, cpp or python, holding a starter bot and the
helper library. The directory defaults to the current one. Maps do not
go inside it: they land in a maps/ folder beside the project, which
every bot of yours can share.

unswbc init python mybot

unswbc maps

Adds any bundled map the folder is missing, and leaves your own files
alone, so run it after upgrading the toolkit. The folder defaults to
maps; name another to choose it, such as unswbc maps ../maps for a
project you made in the current directory.

unswbc maps

unswbc run

Plays one match between two bots on a map and writes a replay. Each bot
is a project directory, rebuilt before it plays, and naming the same one
twice plays it against itself. Add -v to watch every round, or --sandbox
to price a Python bot in CPU points the way the judge does.

unswbc run maps/arena.map mybot mybot

unswbc vscode

Installs the replay viewer into VS Code, Cursor, VSCodium, so any
.replay file opens in the editor. init already does this when it finds
one.

unswbc vscode
